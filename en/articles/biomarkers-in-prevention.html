Biomarkers: what they add (and what they do not) in prevention

Published · 2026-09-25

Today it is easy to get blood work and compare results with internet tables. A biomarker — a measure in blood or another sample — can add useful information, but it does not tell the whole story alone. The same number means different things by age, symptoms, medication and history.

In preventive medicine, markers are chosen with a purpose: to complement clinical assessment, follow a known risk or check whether a habit change trends favourably. Ordering “everything that exists” is rarely the most prudent strategy.

How results are communicated also matters. A value slightly out of range does not always demand alarm; sometimes it is wiser to repeat, contextualise or prioritise another habit before escalating tests. Clinical conversation turns data into decisions.

At Rojas.clinic we guide lab work after listening: what we look for, what we do not, and how it fits your healthy-longevity plan — without rejuvenation promises or miracle readings.
